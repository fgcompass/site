Why it matters

Movement skills are the building blocks of an active, healthy childhood.

Running, jumping, throwing and catching are fundamental movement skills. Children who move well tend to be more physically active, and research links these skills to healthier body composition, stronger thinking skills such as working memory, and more confident play with peers.

Spotting where a child is, and where they are stuck, lets you teach the next step and catch delays early. FG-COMPASS makes that check fast enough to do in a regular class or clinic session.

How it works

Watch, answer, score. That's it.

  1. Watch the child perform a skill

    Observe the skill live, in the gym, playground or clinic. A short protocol covers setup, instructions and trials for each skill.

  2. Answer two yes/no questions

    The first question points toward the lower or upper levels. A second question confirms where the child lands.

  3. Get a level from 1 to 4

    Record the level for each skill to see a child's profile, track progress or compare before and after a program.

The decision tree in action

Every skill uses the same three-question tree. Each child follows one path: a key question, then one confirming question.

 

For researchers: the composite decision-tree model

FG-COMPASS is a process-oriented, criterion-referenced instrument. Each skill is scored with a composite decision tree that combines the Observational Plan and Three-Stage approaches to FMS assessment, using only three key performance criteria drawn from validated and hypothesized developmental sequences.

  • Discriminatory question (DQ) separates the extremes (levels 1 and 4).
  • Upper confirmatory question (UCQ) confirms level 4; if not confirmed, the child is level 3.
  • Lower confirmatory question (LCQ) confirms level 1; if not confirmed, the child is level 2.

Framework model shared by all ten decision trees.

What it measures

Ten skills, two subtests.

Locomotor

Moving the body from place to place.

  • Skipping
  • Hopping
  • Horizontal jump
  • Vertical jump
  • Galloping

Object control

Sending, receiving and controlling a ball.

  • Throwing
  • Catching
  • Kicking
  • Batting
  • Stationary dribble

The evidence

Built on more than 15 years of research.

FG-COMPASS was developed at the University of Pittsburgh and tested for reliability and agreement with expert raters in peer-reviewed studies.
